What I'm researching

Three threads run through my work: the geometry of learned representations, the cost of multimodal inference, and the small engineering choices that decide whether a model is usable in practice. Below are the threads themselves, then the papers that fall out of them.

01 : Interests

  1. Senses for machines

    Most sensors a robot carries produce numbers no model reads. I work on putting those signals into the same space as language, so a pressure array or a thermal frame can be searched with a sentence. The recurring question is how much can be added to a frozen model from the outside, and what it costs to keep every existing embedding exactly as it was. This is the thread behind Fusion Embedding and the sense packs.

  2. Collective decisions under uncertainty

    When a leaderless group has to decide together in a changing world, what should each member attend to, and when is attending at all worth the cost? Extending an existing collective-decision model to asymmetric environments turns up a sharp viability threshold past which the optimal group stops sensing its environment entirely. This is the thread behind the REU at the University of Cincinnati, presented at the 2026 Indiana Undergraduate Math Research Conference.

  3. Representation geometry

    How embedding spaces deform under fine-tuning, quantization, and LoRA adaptation — and whether unlabeled, model-agnostic metrics can predict downstream degradation before it shows up in eval. This is the thread behind SemanticSentry.

  4. Cost-aware multimodal inference

    Practical ways to keep vision-language pipelines cheap: frame deduplication, hierarchical attention budgets, adaptive sampling that preserves signal at a fraction of the API cost. This is the thread behind AdaFrame and the AdLovin pipeline.

  5. Systems for ML at scale

    The plumbing — Rust OCR engines, TensorRT inference, structured downstream analytics — that lets research artifacts move from notebook to production. This is the thread behind my work at Skop Intelligence.

02 : Publications

  1. 2026 arXiv

    Fusion Embedding: A Unified Embedding Space for Text, Image, Video, and Audio

    Tonmoy, A. B., Hoque, K. F., Arham, M. S. I., Luthra, A.

    A technical report on two generations of an open-weight multimodal embedding family. A strong vision-language base is frozen and new modalities are attached from the outside, first through a trained connector and then through modality-gated deep adapters, so text, image and video vectors stay identical to the base model and gaining a modality never forces a re-index. The report covers the training recipe, the corpus decisions that mattered, and the levers that did not work.

  2. 2026 arXiv

    Tactus: Open-Vocabulary Object Recognition from Low-Cost Pressure Arrays

    Tonmoy, A. B.

    Resistive pressure arrays are the cheapest and most widely shipped tactile sensors, yet tactile representation learning has concentrated on optical sensors that image a deforming gel. Tactus answers text queries from pressure data alone: on the STAG benchmark (27 objects, held-out recordings) it reaches 0.771 ± 0.062 top-1 over four runs (top-3 0.935), matching, and at best exceeding, the dataset's supervised closed-set CNN at 0.76, with no trained classifier head.

    contributions

    • A small-data recipe: 187 training recordings, masked-autoencoder pretraining on 144k unlabeled same-sensor frames, and the sensor's own calibration affine, which recovered more accuracy than every architecture change combined.
    • Robustness the deployment story depends on: paraphrased and bare-name queries land within one point, and two diverse frames recover 89% of eight-frame accuracy.
    • Failures reported with equal precision: cross-sensor pretraining pooling gave no gain, matching the field's heterogeneity findings.

  3. 2026 arXiv in submission

    Modality-Gated Deep Adapters: Adding a Modality to a Frozen Embedding Model with Exact Preservation

    Tonmoy, A. B., Hoque, K. F., Arham, M. S. I., Luthra, A.

    Extending a deployed embedding model to a new modality with existing parameter-efficient methods silently changes its outputs, which invalidates every stored embedding. This paper proposes bottleneck adapters grouped into per-modality packs that execute only while their own modality is being encoded. Inputs no pack claims traverse the base model's own computation graph unchanged, and co-loaded packs compose with an exact-zero isolation matrix. Both properties hold after arbitrary training, not only at initialization, and are checked by equality tests on the released checkpoints.

    contributions

    • Modality-gated deep adapters with a machine-checked exactness guarantee, stated as propositions and verified on the released weights.
    • An audio pack that improves audio-to-text retrieval by +3.4 to +5.4 R@10 over an identically trained control, positive at every seed and reproduced at eleven times the data.
    • A thermal pack reusing the base model's own frozen vision path, lifting thermal-to-text retrieval from 0.224 to 0.785.
    • An encoder-swap result locating the missing capacity: an external audio encoder that outranks Whisper-family encoders in CLAP-style comparisons loses by 16 R@10 inside the frozen model.

  4. 2026 arXiv

    Discriminative Axis, Not Data Volume: What a Contrastive Corpus Teaches an Audio Embedding

    Tonmoy, A. B.

    Scaling the corpus is the default remedy when a representation lacks an attribute. This paper reports a case where it does nothing and identifies what does. Adding a lexical-speech round raised zero-shot keyword spotting by 76 points while reducing speech-emotion recognition by 14. The loss is not a capacity limit and not a data-volume problem: 29,428 mined clips whose captions explicitly name emotions move emotion by −0.0007 at matched exposure. A contrastive objective encodes an attribute only when the in-batch negatives cannot be separated without it.

    contributions

    • A clean case where corpus scale fails and corpus structure succeeds, measured on the same audio throughout.
    • A causal intervention: collapsing caption diversity so that emotion becomes the only separating axis recovers it by 8.9 points across three seeds, while raising caption similarity alone does not.
    • A separability statistic computable from the corpus alone, before training, that orders the corpora exactly as their outcomes do.

  5. 2026 BlackboxNLP @ EMNLP under review

    Geometric Drift Metrics are Insufficient: A Matched-Magnitude Dissociation Between Aligned and Anti-Aligned Fine-Tuning

    Tonmoy, A. B., Deng, Q.

    Geometric similarity metrics — Centered Kernel Alignment, neighborhood preservation, isotropy — are widely used to compare neural representations and infer functional similarity. We show this inference fails in two complementary directions. On E5-base-v2, matched-NPS conditions produce order-of-magnitude differences in retrieval damage depending on the fine-tuning objective; on BERT, near-trivial geometric drift coexists with a large, CI-disjoint functional gap between MLM and contrastive fine-tuning on the same corpus. Geometric drift magnitude does not predict the sign or scale of functional change without knowledge of the gradient–pretraining alignment.

    contributions

    • A matched-magnitude dissociation on E5 between gradient-aligned and gradient-anti-aligned fine-tuning at fixed Neighborhood Preservation Score, demonstrated across three seeds and a pre-registered hypothesis.
    • A converse dissociation on BERT where the more-drifted condition is functionally better, opposite to what NPS would predict.
    • A pre-registered corpus control ruling out distribution shift, plus structural controls (matched-Frobenius random rank-4, full-rank fine-tuning) ruling out low-rank confinement as the driver.
    • A linear-probe methodology finding: up to 28.5 pp accuracy swings at identical embeddings under weak vs. cross-validated probe configurations — large enough to contaminate published fine-tuning evaluations.

  6. 2026 ACM Multimedia in review

    AdaFrame: Hierarchical Multimodal Deduplication with Adaptive Information Budgeting for Cost-Efficient Video Advertisement Analysis

    Tonmoy, A. B., Luthra, A., Deng, Q.

    Modern video advertisement analysis pipelines spend most of their compute and API budget on near-duplicate frames. AdaFrame tackles this with a hierarchical multimodal deduplication scheme that allocates an adaptive information budget across frames, prioritizing visual-textual diversity over uniform sampling. On an advertising-video corpus the method achieves 70–90% frame deduplication and reduces downstream vision-API cost by roughly 70% while preserving structured signal extraction.

    contributions

    • Hierarchical deduplication combining CLIP image embeddings with audio-state features from HuBERT.
    • An adaptive per-clip information budget driven by local visual-textual diversity.
    • 70–90% frame deduplication with negligible quality loss on downstream extraction tasks.
